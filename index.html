What is a Stock?

A stock is a piece of a company.

If the business grows, the stock price goes up and you make money. If it struggles, the price drops and you lose money.

2

What is an Option?

An option is a contract.

It's an agreement between two people to buy or sell a stock in the future at an agreed-upon price for a small upfront fee.

THE AGREEMENT
"I am paying you $10 today for the right to buy your stock for exactly $100 anytime before Friday, no matter what the market does.

I can walk away at any time. But either way, my $10 fee is non-refundable."
- The Buyer
THE AGREEMENT
"I accept your $10 today. In exchange, if you decide to buy my stock anytime before Friday, I am forced to sell it to you for exactly $100, no matter what the market does.

Whether you buy the stock or walk away, I get to keep the $10 fee."
- The Seller

There is a strict deadline.

This agreement does not last forever. It is only valid until a specific date, known as the expiration, which is usually a Friday. Once the deadline passes, the contract expires and is no longer active.

The Call Option

A Call locks in a purchase price.

You buy a contract that lets you buy 100 shares at $100 per share ($10,000 total). You pay a $100 Premium to the seller. This is what it means to buy a Call option.

If the stock skyrockets, you profit.

If the stock hits $200 on expiration day, your contract is extremely valuable because you're locked in to buy it at $100.

You now have two choices: buy the shares at a huge discount, or sell the contract itself for a massive profit.

If it crashes, you walk away.

You aren't forced to buy the stock. You just let it expire and lose your initial $100 Premium.

The Put Option

A Put locks in a selling price.

You buy a contract that lets you sell 100 shares at $100 per share ($10,000 total). You pay a $100 Premium to the seller. This is what it means to buy a Put option.

If it crashes, you profit.

If the stock drops to $0 on expiration day, your contract is extremely valuable because you're locked in to sell it at $100.

You now have two choices: sell your shares at a huge markup, or sell the contract itself for a massive profit.

If it skyrockets, you walk away.

You aren't forced to sell the stock. You just let it expire and lose your initial $100 Premium.

Buying vs. Selling

Every contract has a Seller.

So far, we've only talked about buying a Call or a Put, where you pay the premium and pray the stock price moves in your direction.

However you can also be the one selling options contracts (also known as writing options). When you sell, you act like the casino - you collect the Premium upfront, but take on the obligation if the buyer wins.

The Strike Zone

The Casino's Risk

How do you know if the buyer wins? It's all about the Strike Price.

If the stock crosses past the strike price, it is now "in" the risk zone. We call this being In The Money (ITM). The buyer will use the contract, and you are forced to fulfill it (by buying or selling the shares). This is called being Assigned.

Otherwise, the contract is Out of The Money (OTM) and expires harmlessly. You keep the Premium and walk away.

The Secret: Assignment isn't a loss.

Depending on if it's a Call or a Put, the ITM risk zone flips (try the toggle above!).

But here is the greatest secret of selling options: Getting assigned doesn't mean you lose money.

If you get assigned on a Put, you are simply forced to buy a stock at a discount. If you get assigned on a Call, you are simply forced to sell your stock for a guaranteed profit. Your only "loss" is missing out on potential further gains (opportunity cost).

7

The Loop

The Casino's Playbook

We don't buy options. We act like the casino, sell them to other people, and collect the Premiums in a continuous loop.

Step 1: Get paid to wait.

Sell a Put on a stock you love. Keep collecting Premiums on repeat until...

Step 2: Buy the shares on sale.

...the stock drops. You get Assigned and buy the shares at a discount.

Step 3: Get paid to rent them out.

Sell a Call against your new shares. Keep collecting Premiums on repeat until...

Step 4: Sell them for a profit.

...the stock jumps. You are Assigned and forced to sell your shares for a guaranteed profit. The loop restarts back at Step 1!
